Compiled findings across five research categories. Each entry includes Title, URL, key quote or finding, and category.

## Category 1: John C. Lilly "Human Biocomputer" in Modern NLP/AI Context

### Source 1.1
**Title:** Programming and Metaprogramming in the Human Biocomputer: Theory and Experiments (Primary Source)
**URL:** https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20eac974952088d7df474b5d312768fa6651765f
**Category:** Scholar
**Key Finding:** The foundational text (1968/1972) in which Lilly proposes the brain-as-hardware / mind-as-software / consciousness-beyond-both tripartite model. Lilly coined the term "human biocomputer" to describe the totality of human anatomy as a self-programming, self-metaprogramming system. He describes the "selfmetaprogrammer" as the executive "I" within the mind — analogous to what modern metacognitive AI architectures call a monitoring or control meta-process. The concept of nested levels (program → metaprogram → self-metaprogram) maps structurally onto modern multi-level cognitive architectures. Lilly explicitly connected his ideas to ENIAC-era computing after contact with Penn's Moore School engineers. As the Substack analysis of the text notes: "We are both the metaprogrammer and the metaprogramee — Lilly's 'selfmetaprogrammer'... the executive functions... performing the higher-order operations that are now central to metacognitive AI."
**Notes:** Direct precursor text. No modern AI paper cites Lilly directly, but the structural homology is significant and documented in secondary scholarship on the history of cognitive science.

### Source 1.2
**Title:** Metacognitive AI: Framework and the Case for a Neurosymbolic Approach
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12147
**Category:** Scholar
**Key Finding:** Wei, Shakarian, Lebiere et al. (2024) introduce the TRAP framework for metacognitive AI: **T**ransparency, **R**easoning, **A**daptation, **P**erception. The paper argues that neurosymbolic AI (NSAI) is uniquely suited to implement metacognition — reasoning about an agent's own internal processes. This maps directly onto Lilly's "selfmetaprogrammer": a system-level process that monitors and modulates lower-order programs. Quote: "Metacognition is the concept of reasoning about an agent's own internal processes and was originally introduced in the field of developmental psychology. We explore how neurosymbolic AI (NSAI) can be leveraged to address challenges of metacognition."
**DOI:** 10.48550/arXiv.2406.12147

---

### Source 1.3
**Title:** From Internal Models Toward Metacognitive AI
**URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8551129/
**Category:** Scholar
**Key Finding:** Kawato & Cortese (2021) propose the Cognitive Reality Monitoring Network (CRMN) — a prefrontal executive system that orchestrates consciousness and metacognition via "responsibility signals" across parallel internal model pairs. The architecture is structurally analogous to Lilly's nested metaprogramming levels: lower-level generative-inverse model pairs correspond to Lilly's "programs," while CRMN's executive monitoring corresponds to the "selfmetaprogrammer." Quote: "Metacognition — the ability to monitor one's own thoughts — may be part of the solution to the enigma of learning large-dimensional problems with few trials... The model could lead to new-generation AI exhibiting metacognition, consciousness, dimension reduction, selection of modules and corresponding representations."
**DOI:** 10.1007/s00422-021-00904-7

---

### Source 1.4
**Title:** How Metacognitive Architectures Remember Their Own Thoughts: A Systematic Review
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13467
**Category:** Scholar
**Key Finding:** Nolte et al. (2025) systematically review 35 Computational Metacognitive Architectures (CMAs) featuring introspective data ranging from "symbolic event traces to sub-symbolic arousal metrics." The review maps exactly onto Lilly's hierarchical metaprogramming: lower-order event traces (programs) monitored by higher-order executive systems (metaprograms). The review finds CMAs leverage metacognitive experiences for "error diagnosis, self-repair, and goal-driven learning" — language that parallels Lilly's account of the selfmetaprogrammer correcting and reprogramming the biocomputer. Key finding: "Our findings highlight both the promise of metacognitive experiences — in boosting adaptability, explainability, and overall system performance — and the persistent lack of shared [standards]."
**DOI:** 10.48550/arXiv.2503.13467

### Source 1.5 (Social/Web)
**Title:** Book Review: Programming and Meta-Programming in the Human Biocomputer (Hard Problems, Substack)
**URL:** https://tobeabat.substack.com/p/book-review-programming-and-meta
**Category:** Social (Substack, serious secondary analysis)
**Key Finding:** A technically literate secondary analysis by a computer science practitioner tracing the direct genealogy from ENIAC to Lilly's theory: "In the foreword to the second edition of Biocomputer, Lilly writes: 'I coupled these ideas back to my own software through the atmosphere of my neurophysiological research on cerebral cortex. It was more fully elaborated in the tank isolation solitude and confinement work at NIMH from 1953 to 1958.'" The review maps Lilly's metaprogramming levels onto modern neural network architectures and notes that "most, if not all, of the myriad developments in the field of artificial intelligence, all the way back to the lowly perceptron, take their inspiration from our own nervous system." Provides concrete connection between Lilly's historical context and contemporary ML.

## Category 2: Iatrogenic Psychiatric Harm from Risperidone in Adolescents

### Source 2.1
**Title:** Comparative Effects of Antipsychotics on Metabolic and Endocrine Function in Children and Young People With Schizophrenia: A Systematic Review and Network Meta-Analysis
**URL:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/identifier/S2056472423002314/type/journal_article
**Category:** Scholar (Systematic Review / Network Meta-Analysis)
**Key Finding:** Rogdaki, McCutcheon, Howes & Pillinger (2023, BJPsych Open). The most methodologically rigorous comparative study: 15 RCTs, 2,501 patients, 12 antipsychotics. Risperidone/paliperidone produced the largest prolactin elevation of all drugs: **mean difference +28.10 ng/mL** (95% CI 16.23–39.96) vs. placebo. Risperidone also produced the largest HDL cholesterol reduction (−0.23 mmol/L). Authors conclude: "Treatment guidelines should be updated to reflect our findings. However, the choice of antipsychotic should be made on an individual basis, considering the clinical circumstances and preferences of young people, carers, and clinicians."
**DOI:** 10.1192/bjo.2023.231

---

### Source 2.2
**Title:** Cardiometabolic Adverse Effects of Long-Term Antipsychotic Treatment in Children and Adolescents with Non-Psychotic Disorders: A Systematic Review
**URL:** https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s00787-025-02771-0
**Category:** Scholar (Systematic Review, 2025)
**Key Finding:** Radha Krishnan et al. (2025, European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry). Most recent systematic review, 15 observational studies, N=114,141, mean age 10.9 years. Risperidone was examined in 80% of studies (12/15). Long-term antipsychotic treatment was associated with: weight gain (91.6% of studies), hyperglycaemia (100%), dyslipidaemia (66.6%), metabolic syndrome (100% of studies measuring it). Quote: "Prolonged exposure to antipsychotics during childhood may be associated with several metabolic adverse effects that could have long-term health impacts... Antipsychotic prescribing for children should be assessed on an individual basis, after weighing the benefits of therapy against the harms that may arise."
**DOI:** 10.1007/s00787-025-02771-0

---

### Source 2.3
**Title:** Risperidone Induced Hyperprolactinemia: From Basic to Clinical Studies
**URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9121093/
**Category:** Scholar (Narrative Review)
**Key Finding:** Radmanovic, Stojković, Jovanovic et al. (2022, Frontiers in Psychiatry). Comprehensive review of the mechanism and clinical cascade: risperidone's D2 blockade in the tuberoinfundibular pathway disinhibits prolactin secretion at rates comparable to haloperidol — higher than any other atypical antipsychotic. Quote: "It has been frequently shown that the side effects of Risperidone are the most common cause of non-compliance with therapy, resulting in **worsening of psychiatric symptoms and hospitalization**." This is the iatrogenic loop: hyperprolactinemia → non-compliance → symptom relapse. Prevalence in pediatric samples ranges 43.2%–64%+.
**DOI:** 10.3389/fpsyt.2022.874705

---

### Source 2.4
**Title:** The Short-Term Effects of Risperidone-Induced Hyperprolactinemia on Lipid Metabolism in Drug-Naïve Children and Adolescents
**URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4310921/
**Category:** Scholar
**Key Finding:** Park & Park (2015). Cross-sectional study of 25 drug-naïve children and adolescents (ages 7–18) on risperidone for ≥3 months. Measures include prolactin, thyroid, and lipid profiles. Demonstrates that elevated prolactin in drug-naïve children is directly linked to risperidone exposure and triggers downstream metabolic disruption — establishing the iatrogenic chain in the most vulnerable population (first-episode, treatment-naïve).
**DOI:** 10.4306/pi.2015.12.1.55

---

### Source 2.5
**Title:** Metabolic Side Effects of Risperidone in Pediatric Patients with Neurological Disorders: A Prospective Cohort Study
**URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11432673/
**Category:** Scholar (Prospective Cohort)
**Key Finding:** Albaker et al. (2024, Journal of Clinical Medicine). Prospective cohort of children with neurological disorders prescribed risperidone. Finding: 53.8% of children experienced at least one metabolic side effect; hyperlipidemia most common (34.6%); prolactin levels elevated. Quote: "Monitoring metabolic and anthropometric parameters in children receiving risperidone for neurological disorders is cardinal. Clinicians should consider individualized treatment plans, closely [monitoring adverse effects]." Notably, neurological disorder patients are a population where cognitive effects of metabolic disruption could be especially severe and could mimic or exacerbate baseline psychiatric presentations.
**DOI:** 10.3390/jcm13185565

### Source 2.6
**Title:** Antipsychotic Medication in Children and Adolescents: A Descriptive Review of the Effects on Prolactin Level and Associated Side Effects
**URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1089/cap.2008.0120
**Category:** Scholar (Descriptive Review)
**Key Finding:** Roke, van Harten, Boot & Buitelaar (2009, Journal of Child and Adolescent Psychopharmacology). Review specifically focused on prolactin-related side effects in children: gynecomastia, galactorrhea, amenorrhea, sexual dysfunction, and potential bone density loss. Establishes that prolactin elevation in developing adolescents has cascading neuroendocrine consequences that may worsen overall psychiatric profile through: (a) body image disturbance triggering anxiety/depression, (b) sexual dysfunction increasing social withdrawal, (c) non-compliance triggering symptom relapse.
**DOI:** 10.1089/cap.2008.0120

## Category 3: Czech Psychiatric Reform and CRPD Compliance

### Source 3.1
**Title:** Adherence to the Convention on the Rights of People with Disabilities in Czech Psychiatric Hospitals (PMC / Health and Human Rights)
**URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7348437/
**Category:** Scholar/Web
**Key Finding:** Nationwide systematic assessment of all 17 Czech psychiatric hospitals using WHO QualityRights Toolkit (2017–2019, 579 interviews). Finding: **none of the CRPD articles assessed were fully adhered to** in any Czech psychiatric hospital. Particular deficits: de-escalation methods absent in 11/17 hospitals; recovery plans absent in 11/17; rights-based training absent in 11/17; restraint and seclusion inconsistent with CRPD in most facilities. Authors note: "The use of restraint and seclusion measures is particularly problematic. Most staff lack training in de-escalation techniques, and patient-preferred methods of intervention during crisis are not taken into account."
**DOI:** Health and Human Rights Journal (2020)

---

### Source 3.2
**Title:** Czechia: ECHR Finds Violation of Right to Life of Young Man Tasered in Psychiatric Hospital
**URL:** https://validity.ngo/czechia-european-court-of-human-rights-finds-violation-of-right-to-life-of-young-man-tasered-in-psychiatric-hospital/
**Category:** Web (Validity Foundation)
**Key Finding:** Landmark 2023 ECHR ruling (V. v. Czech Republic). First ever finding that Czech Republic violated Article 2 ECHR (right to life) due to psychiatric hospital restraint practices. P.Z., 30, was compulsorily admitted to Olomouc University Hospital's psychiatric clinic, administered antipsychotics without consent, threatened with seclusion, restrained in prone position by police, tasered three times, given two antipsychotic injections — and died from presumed cardiac arrest. Court found hospital should have used de-escalation, not police force. Quote (Validity Litigation Director): "The Czech psychiatric system was entirely unprepared. No services with well-trained multidisciplinary crisis teams were available, as they would be in other countries. The implementation of the judgment will require heightened efforts on the part of the Czech Ministry of Health to invest in quality, community-based services."

---

### Source 3.3
**Title:** Sýkora v. Czech Republic (ECHR 2012)
**URL:** https://www.globalhealthrights.org/sykora-v-czech-republic/
**Category:** Web (Global Health Rights Database)
**Key Finding:** Sýkora was admitted to Brno-Černovice Psychiatric Hospital against his will on a guardian's consent — the guardian had never met him. Court found violations of Article 5.1 (right to liberty), 5.4 (judicial review of detention), and Article 8 (private and family life). Key holding: "A procedure which merely required the guardian's consent to the admission to such an institution did not provide sufficient safeguards against arbitrariness." Sýkora had warned doctors during admission that "neuroleptic psychiatric medication had a negative [effect on him]" — his testimony was disregarded. The case directly illustrates the intersection of forced treatment and epistemic injustice.

### Source 3.4
**Title:** Learning from Mental Health Reform Under Political Change (Czech Case Study)
**URL:** https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09540261.2026.2619456
**Category:** Web/Scholar
**Key Finding:** Comprehensive 2026 policy analysis of Czechia's ESIF-funded psychiatric reform (2017–2022). Key outcomes: inpatient census fell from 8,072 (2018) to 6,512 (2022); 29 community mental health centres established; involvement of people with lived experience (PWLE) increased. However, the paper warns: "several components remained dependent on temporary arrangements or fragmented financing structures, which became increasingly visible following the end of the ESIF programming period and subsequent political change." Reform is real but fragile.

## Category 4: Neuro-Symbolic AI as Metacognitive Monitoring System

### Source 4.1
**Title:** Metacognitive AI: Framework and the Case for a Neurosymbolic Approach
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12147
**Category:** Scholar
**Key Finding:** (See also Category 1.) Wei et al. (2024). Explicitly proposes neurosymbolic AI as the architecture of choice for metacognition. The TRAP framework (Transparency, Reasoning, Adaptation, Perception) maps each metacognitive function onto a neurosymbolic capability. Transparency = symbolic interpretability; Reasoning = formal inference over neural states; Adaptation = learning from experience; Perception = monitoring internal representations. The paper is the most direct academic argument for neurosymbolic AI as metacognitive monitoring infrastructure.
**DOI:** 10.48550/arXiv.2406.12147

---

### Source 4.2
**Title:** Metagent-P: A Neuro-Symbolic Planning Agent with Metacognition for Open Worlds
**URL:** https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1169
**Category:** Scholar (ACL 2025)
**Key Finding:** Zhou et al. (2025). A working implementation of a neuro-symbolic metacognitive agent in Minecraft open-world tasks. Architecture: "planning-verification-execution-reflection" — four-phase cycle where symbolic reasoning verifies neural planning outputs, and a metacognitive reflection module monitors and corrects plan failures. Quote: "Drawing inspiration from human cognitive-metacognitive collaboration, we propose Metagent-P, integrating the world knowledge of LLMs, the symbolic reasoning capabilities of cognitive architectures, and the self-reflection characteristic of metacognition... it actively adapts the agent to dynamic environments through monitoring, evaluation, and regulation mechanisms." Results: 34% reduction in replanning, 18.96% above human success rate on long-horizon tasks.
**DOI:** 10.18653/v1/2025.findings-acl.1169

---

### Source 4.3
**Title:** Neuro-Symbolic AI for Patient Monitoring
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11995
**Category:** Scholar
**Key Finding:** Fenske, Bader & Kirste (2024). Directly applies NeSy-AI to patient monitoring — a domain-specific metacognitive monitoring use case. Paper argues that Human Activity Recognition (HAR) for patient monitoring requires symbolic constraints to handle clinical reasoning that pure neural approaches cannot provide. Quote: "We argue that Neuro-Symbolic AI (NeSy-AI) should be applied for patient monitoring... we present a process architecture and discuss why NeSy-AI should be applied for patient monitoring. To further support our argumentation, we show how NeSy-AI can help to overcome certain technical challenges that arise from this application area." This provides a direct bridge between neuro-symbolic metacognition and clinical/psychiatric monitoring.
**DOI:** 10.48550/arXiv.2407.11995

---

### Source 4.4
**Title:** GRACE: A Reason-Based Neuro-Symbolic Architecture for Safe and Ethical AI Alignment
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.10520
**Category:** Scholar (2026)
**Key Finding:** Jahn et al. (2026). Introduces GRACE, a neuro-symbolic containment architecture with a dedicated **Guard** module that monitors and enforces normative compliance — a structural metacognitive monitor over an AI agent's decision-making. Critically, the paper demonstrates GRACE on a **LLM therapy assistant** example: "We demonstrate GRACE on an example of a LLM therapy assistant, showing how it enables stakeholders to understand, contest, and refine agent behavior." The Guard module uses deontic logic-based symbolic reasoning to constrain neural agent actions — precisely the kind of metacognitive oversight system Lilly's framework anticipates for any self-programming system.
**DOI:** 10.48550/arXiv.2601.10520

---

### Source 4.5
**Title:** How Metacognitive Architectures Remember Their Own Thoughts: A Systematic Review
**URL:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13467
**Category:** Scholar
**Key Finding:** Nolte et al. (2025). Systematic review of 35 Computational Metacognitive Architectures (CMAs) specifically examining how systems store and use introspective data for error diagnosis and self-repair. Covers architectures ranging from fully symbolic to hybrid neuro-symbolic. Key finding: CMAs using symbolic event traces (i.e., neuro-symbolic approaches) showed the greatest advantage in interpretability and formal verification of self-monitoring. Directly relevant as a landscape survey of the field.
**DOI:** 10.48550/arXiv.2503.13467

## Category 5: Epistemic Injustice in Psychiatric Diagnosis

### Source 5.1
**Title:** Epistemic Injustice in Psychiatry
**URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5376720/
**Category:** Scholar (Landmark editorial)
**Key Finding:** Carel, Kidd & Crichton (2017, BJPsych Bulletin). The foundational application of Miranda Fricker's framework to psychiatry. Key claims: "People with mental disorders are even more vulnerable to epistemic injustice than those with somatic illnesses, owing to the high prevalence and great power of negative stereotypes of psychiatric illness." Testimonial injustice occurs when psychiatrists unconsciously deflate patient credibility: "the patient may be telling the truth, but the doctor deflates the level of credibility which she gives to the patient ('credibility deficit') and thereby does the patient a distinctive kind of injustice, namely epistemic injustice." Concrete examples include a patient whose true claim about being a Soviet leader's relative was dismissed as a delusion.
**DOI:** 10.1192/pb.bp.115.050682

---

### Source 5.2
**Title:** Epistemic Injustice and Psychiatric Classification
**URL:** https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/identifier/S0031824800015464/type/journal_article
**Category:** Scholar
**Key Finding:** Bueter (2019, Philosophy of Science). Introduces the concept of "preemptive testimonial injustice" — injustice that precedes testimony by excluding patients from diagnostic decision-making on grounds of presumed irrelevance or lack of expertise. Argues this exclusion is itself epistemically unjust because: (1) values play an ineliminable role in psychiatric classification, and (2) first-person knowledge is a necessary corrective to value-laden diagnostic criteria. Directly applicable to situations where adolescent patients' reports of adverse medication effects (e.g., prolactin-related symptoms) are preemptively dismissed.
**DOI:** 10.1086/705443

---

### Source 5.3
**Title:** Antipsychotic Discontinuation Through the Lens of Epistemic Injustice
**URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11772521/
**Category:** Scholar
**Key Finding:** Roe, Speyer & Eplov (2024, Community Mental Health Journal). Applies Fricker's framework specifically to the clinical scenario of patients wishing to stop antipsychotic medication "against medical advice." Quote: "We argue that epistemic injustice is a useful concept to understand and discuss situations in which clients and medical professionals disagree about decisions to stop or maintain treatment with antipsychotic medication." Directly bridges Categories 2 and 5: when adolescents (or their parents) report adverse risperidone effects and request discontinuation, testimonial injustice may result in their concerns being systematically discounted.
**DOI:** 10.1007/s10597-024-01274-7

---

### Source 5.4
**Title:** Scaling Injustice: Epistemic Harm in DID and What Clinical Records Will Teach AI
**URL:** https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2026.1741240/full
**Category:** Scholar (2026)
**Key Finding:** Akinlade (2026, Frontiers in Psychiatry). Examines how testimonial and hermeneutical injustices in psychiatric diagnosis become embedded in clinical records that then train AI systems — creating an "algorithmic amplification of longstanding harms." Quote: "If clinicians cannot recognize or document complex trauma accurately, automated systems will scale these failures exponentially." Directly links epistemic injustice to AI systems and is thus highly relevant to both Category 5 and the AI context of Categories 1 and 4.
**DOI:** 10.3389/fpsyt.2026.1741240

---

### Source 5.5
**Title:** Objectivity, Standardization, and Epistemic Injustice in Psychiatry
**URL:** https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s11229-025-05016-4
**Category:** Scholar (2025)
**Key Finding:** Ballesteros (2025, Synthese). Argues that psychiatry's reliance on standardized instruments (DSM, rating scales) structurally produces epistemic injustice by treating patients as "mere informants or, worse, as sources of information." Quote: "Understanding objectivity as standardization promotes treating patients as mere informants... which may underpin various forms of epistemic injustice, such as hermeneutical or contributory injustice." In an adolescent context, this is especially potent: standardized rating scales may not capture medication-induced symptom changes that only the patient can report.
**DOI:** 10.1007/s11229-025-05016-4

---

### Source 5.6
**Title:** Psychiatrization, Assertions of Epistemic Justice, and the Question of Agency
**URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9947832/
**Category:** Scholar
**Key Finding:** Russo (2023, Frontiers in Sociology). First-person survivor perspective critiquing the medicalized framing of epistemic injustice in psychiatry. Key argument: "I critique the practice of attributing testimonial injustice solely to the 'stigma against mental illness', and point to psychiatric diagnosing itself as a principal enabler and re-producer of this form of injustice." Uniquely positions diagnosis — not merely attitude — as the instrument of epistemic harm. Provides critical counter-perspective to clinical-academic framings and connects to CRPD-aligned survivor voices.
**DOI:** 10.3389/fsoc.2023.1092298
